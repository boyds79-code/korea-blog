Seoul's subway system carries over **8 million passengers daily** across 23 lines and more than 700 stations—one of the world's most extensive networks. For foreign visitors, the good news is that navigating it is surprisingly easy once you've downloaded the right app. The challenge? Korea has several competing transit apps, and not all are created equal for English-speaking tourists.

This guide breaks down the best subway apps available in Korea, compares their strengths and quirks, and shows you exactly which one to download before your trip.

## Why You Need a Dedicated Korean Transit App

Google Maps works in many countries, but in Korea it's **severely limited** for transit navigation. Due to South Korean mapping restrictions, Google can't provide turn-by-turn walking directions or accurate real-time subway routes.

You'll need a Korean-made app that has full access to local mapping data. The two dominant players—Naver and Kakao—both offer robust English support and offline capabilities that make them infinitely more useful than trying to fumble through with Google.

Most of these apps are free, work offline once routes are cached, and integrate subway, bus, and walking directions into a single search.

## Naver Map: The All-Rounder for Most Tourists

**Naver Map** is Korea's most comprehensive navigation app, and for most foreign visitors it's the single best choice to install.

![Screenshot comparison of Korean subway navigation apps](/images/blog/best-korean-subway-apps-for-foreign-tourists-2024-guide/2.jpg)

### What Makes Naver Map Stand Out

Naver offers complete English-language support, covering not just subway lines but also buses, taxis, walking routes, and even restaurant/shop locations. When you search for a subway route, you'll see:

- **Multiple route options** with transfer details, travel time, and cost
- **Real-time train arrival** information at each platform
- First and last train times for every station
- Exit numbers with street-level directions to your final destination

The exit guidance is particularly valuable in Seoul, where major stations like Gangnam or Seoul Station have **12+ exits** that can deposit you blocks apart.

### Setup Tips for Tourists

Download Naver Map (green icon with a white "N") before you arrive. Switch the language to English in settings, and enable location services.

You don't need a Korean phone number to use navigation features. However, creating a free Naver account (possible with an international email) lets you save favorite locations and access more features like reviews.

> 💡 **Tip:** Naver Map shows **which subway car** to board for the fastest transfer or exit—look for the small colored dots on route details.

## Kakao Metro: Subway-Only Simplicity

If you want a laser-focused subway app without the extra features, **Kakao Metro** (formerly Subway Korea) is clean, fast, and excellent for quick route checks.

### What It Does Well

Kakao Metro specializes in subway-only navigation. The interface is simpler than Naver Map, with large, easy-to-read route cards showing:

- Transfer stations highlighted clearly
- Estimated travel time and distance
- Real-time train positions on some lines
- Offline mode that works without data

The app covers Seoul, Busan, Daegu, Gwangju, Daejeon, and other cities with subway systems—helpful if you're traveling beyond the capital.

### Where It Falls Short

Kakao Metro won't help you navigate from your subway exit to a specific restaurant or hotel; it ends at the station. You'll need a second app (like Kakao Map or Naver Map) for the last-mile directions.

It also lacks bus integration, so if the fastest route involves a bus leg, you won't see it.

## Comparing Your Top Options

Here's how the main apps stack up for foreign tourists:

| App | English Support | Offline Mode | Bus Routes | Walking Directions | Best For |
|-----|-----------------|--------------|------------|-------------------|----------|
| **Naver Map** | Full | Partial | Yes | Yes | All-in-one navigation |
| **Kakao Metro** | Full | Yes | No | No | Subway-only trips |
| **Kakao Map** | Full | Partial | Yes | Yes | Alternative to Naver |
| **Google Maps** | Full | Yes | Limited | No turn-by-turn | Not recommended |

**Kakao Map** (the yellow speech-bubble icon) is Naver's main competitor and works similarly well for most tourists. Choose based on personal preference—both are excellent. Many locals use Kakao Map because it integrates with Kakao Taxi.

## Practical Features That Matter on the Ground

### Real-Time Arrivals and Delays

Both Naver Map and Kakao Metro show **live train arrivals** down to the minute. During rush hour or weekend engineering work, this feature is invaluable for deciding whether to wait or find an alternate route.

Seoul's subway is exceptionally punctual, but delays do happen—especially on older lines like Line 2 during peak commute times (8–9 AM, 6–7 PM).

### Station Facilities and Exit Information

Naver Map displays which exits have **elevators and escalators**, critical if you're traveling with luggage or strollers. Not all Seoul subway stations are fully accessible—some older stops have only stairs.

You can also see which exits lead to specific landmarks, bus stops, or neighborhoods, saving you from walking in circles at street level.

## Setting Up Before You Arrive

Download your chosen app on WiFi before your flight. Both Naver Map and Kakao Metro work on iOS and Android and have small file sizes (under 200 MB).

**You will need mobile data or WiFi** for real-time features and initial route searches, though routes cache for offline reference. Most tourists either:

- Buy a Korean tourist SIM card at the airport (around ₩20,000–30,000 for 5–10 days of data)
- Rent a portable WiFi device (₩5,000–7,000 per day)
- Use free WiFi at subway stations (available but not always reliable)

Once you've searched a route, you can screenshot or favorite it for offline access in tunnels.

> ⚠️ **Note:** Korean apps often request permissions (location, notifications) that seem aggressive by Western standards—this is normal and required for full functionality.

## Beyond the Apps: Payment Essentials

No app can buy your ticket, so grab a **T-money** or **Cashbee card** (₩2,500–4,000) at any convenience store. These rechargeable tap cards work on all subways and buses nationwide and save you about ₩100 per ride versus single-journey tickets.

You can also use Samsung Pay or certain credit cards with NFC, but a physical T-money card is simpler and works as a backup if your phone dies.

## The Bottom Line

For most foreign tourists, **Naver Map is the best single app** to download—it handles subway, bus, walking, and destination search in one package with full English support. Install it, allow location access, and you're set for navigating all of Korea.

If you prefer a streamlined, subway-only tool, **Kakao Metro** is excellent for quick route checks and works beautifully offline.

Either way, you'll find Korea's subway system is one of the easiest in the world to navigate—clean, affordable (most trips cost ₩1,400–2,500), and punctual. With the right app in hand, you'll be zipping around Seoul like a local within hours of landing.
