Mayak gyeran—literally "drug eggs"—earned their name because they're dangerously addictive. This is one of Korea's most beloved 밥도둑 (*bap-doduk*, "rice thief") side dishes, the kind that makes you reach for spoonful after spoonful of plain rice. Popular among college students and solo diners, these soft-boiled eggs sit in a salty-sweet soy marinade in your fridge, ready to save any meal or late-night craving. They're meal-prep gold: make a batch, store them in a jar, and you've got instant flavor for a week.

## Boil the Eggs

Bring a medium pot of water to a **rolling boil** over high heat. Gently lower the eggs into the boiling water using a spoon to prevent cracking. Set a timer for **exactly 6½ to 7 minutes** for soft, jammy yolks—this is the key texture. If you prefer firmer yolks, go up to 8 minutes, but the soft, golden center is what makes these eggs special.

While the eggs cook, prepare an ice bath: fill a large bowl with cold water and plenty of ice. As soon as the timer goes off, transfer the eggs immediately to the ice bath. Let them sit for **at least 5 minutes** to stop the cooking and make peeling much easier.

## Peel the Eggs

Gently tap each egg all over on the counter, then roll it lightly to crack the shell evenly. Peel under running water or in the ice bath—the water helps slide the shell off cleanly. **Soft-boiled eggs are delicate**, so peel slowly to avoid tearing the whites. Set the peeled eggs aside.

## Make the Marinade

In a small saucepan, combine the soy sauce, water, rice vinegar, and sugar. Heat over medium heat, stirring occasionally, until the sugar dissolves completely—**about 2-3 minutes**. You don't need to boil it; just warm it enough to melt the sugar. Taste: it should be salty, a little sweet, and tangy. Remove from heat and let it cool to room temperature.

## Assemble and Marinate

Place the peeled eggs in a clean glass jar or airtight container. Add the sliced garlic, green onions, and chili peppers around the eggs. Pour the cooled marinade over everything until the eggs are mostly submerged. If they float, that's fine—just turn them once or twice during marinating.

Cover and refrigerate for **at least 2 hours**, but overnight is even better. The eggs will turn a beautiful amber color and soak up the savory-sweet flavor. **You can keep them in the fridge for up to 5 days**; they'll get saltier and more flavorful as they sit.

> 💡 **Tip:** Don't throw away the marinade when the eggs are gone—use it to quick-pickle sliced cucumbers, radish, or onions, or drizzle it over rice for extra flavor.

## Serving and Variations

Serve the eggs halved or whole over a bowl of hot white rice, spooned with a little marinade and the pickled garlic and peppers. Sprinkle with sesame seeds and a few drops of sesame oil if you like. They're also fantastic tucked into a lunchbox, sliced onto instant noodles, or eaten straight from the jar as a snack.

**Can't find rice vinegar?** White vinegar or apple cider vinegar work fine; reduce the amount by a teaspoon if using distilled white vinegar, as it's sharper. **No fresh chili?** Use a pinch of red pepper flakes or skip it—the dish is still delicious. Some cooks add a splash of mirin or corn syrup for extra sweetness, but plain sugar is traditional and easier to find.

These eggs are proof that the simplest recipes can be the most crave-worthy. Make a jar this weekend and watch it disappear.
