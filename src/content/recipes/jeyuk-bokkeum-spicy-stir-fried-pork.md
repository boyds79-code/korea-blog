Jeyuk bokkeum is one of those dishes that seems deceptively simple until you taste it done right—sweet, spicy, savory, with pork that's tender inside and caramelized at the edges. The secret isn't exotic technique; it's **marinating the meat properly** and knowing how to manage your heat so the sauce clings and glazes instead of steaming the meat into gray sadness. This is weeknight-friendly food that tastes like you know what you're doing.

![Raw pork and marinade ingredients](/images/recipes/jeyuk-bokkeum-spicy-stir-fried-pork/ingredients.jpg)

## Marinate the Pork (Essential Step)

In a large bowl, combine the gochujang, gochugaru, soy sauce, sugar, honey, sesame oil, minced garlic, and ginger. Mix until the sugar dissolves and everything forms a thick, deep red paste. Add the sliced pork and massage the marinade into every piece with your hands—this isn't the time to be dainty. **Let it sit for at least 30 minutes at room temperature, or up to 4 hours in the fridge**.

This marinating time (밑간 시간) is non-negotiable if you want flavor *in* the meat, not just *on* it. The gochujang and sugar need time to penetrate the pork, and the enzymes in the garlic and ginger start to tenderize the protein. If you're pressed for time, 30 minutes will work, but an hour or two is noticeably better.

> 💡 **Tip:** Pork shoulder is leaner and more economical than belly, but belly gives you richer flavor and those irresistible crispy fat edges. Use what you prefer or can find—both work beautifully.

## Prep Your Vegetables

While the pork marinates, slice the onion into roughly ½-inch-thick pieces and cut the green onions into 2-inch segments. You want them sturdy enough to hold up to high heat without turning to mush. Set them aside—you'll add them partway through cooking so they stay crisp-tender, not soggy.

## Stir-Fry Over High Heat

Heat a large skillet or wok over **high heat** until it's properly hot—a drop of water should sizzle and evaporate immediately. Add the neutral oil and swirl to coat. Working in batches if your pan isn't huge, add the marinated pork in a single layer. **Let it sit undisturbed for 45-60 seconds** before stirring. This is the key to avoiding sticking (눌러붙지 않게): if you stir too early, the marinade will glue the meat to the pan. Once the bottom caramelizes and releases naturally, toss and stir-fry for **3-4 minutes** until the pork is mostly cooked through and the edges start to char.

Add the sliced onion and stir-fry for another **2 minutes**, letting it soften slightly and pick up color. The onion will release a little moisture, which helps deglaze any sticky bits and keeps everything from burning. Finally, toss in the green onions and cook for **1 more minute**—just long enough to take the raw edge off but keep them bright and snappy.

## Finishing Touches

The sauce should be thick, glossy, and clinging to every piece of pork and vegetable. If it looks too dry, add a tablespoon of water; if it's too wet and pooling, crank the heat and let it reduce for 30 seconds. Transfer to a serving plate and sprinkle with sesame seeds if you like.

## Serving and Substitutions

Serve jeyuk bokkeum with steamed rice, lettuce or perilla leaves for wraps (ssam), and a few banchan. Kimchi and a simple cucumber salad are classic partners. If you can't find gochujang, there's no perfect substitute, but a mix of miso, sriracha, and a pinch of sugar can get you partway there—though I'd encourage ordering gochujang online, since it keeps for months and unlocks a dozen other Korean dishes. For a milder version, dial back the gochugaru or skip it entirely; the gochujang alone still gives you plenty of flavor and a gentle kick.

Leftovers reheat beautifully and make an excellent filling for kimbap, fried rice, or even tacos if you're feeling adventurous.
