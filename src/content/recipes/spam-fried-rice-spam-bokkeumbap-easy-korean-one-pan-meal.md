Spam bokkeumbap is the ultimate Korean comfort food for busy nights, solo dinners, or when the fridge is nearly empty. While many Korean fried rice recipes rely on kimchi for flavor, this version doesn't need it at all—it's built around the salty, savory richness of Spam (hugely popular in Korea since the Korean War), balanced with aromatic garlic and the nutty finish of sesame oil. Korean students, office workers, and home cooks love this dish because it's fast, satisfying, and uses pantry staples you probably already have.

![Raw ingredients for spam fried rice](/images/recipes/spam-fried-rice-spam-bokkeumbap-easy-korean-one-pan-meal/ingredients.jpg)

## Prep Your Rice and Ingredients

The secret to great fried rice is **cold, day-old rice**. Freshly cooked rice is too moist and sticky—it will clump and turn mushy when stir-fried. If you only have fresh rice, spread it on a plate and refrigerate uncovered for at least 30 minutes to dry it out. Break up any clumps with your hands before cooking.

Dice the Spam into **1/2-inch cubes**—not too small or they'll crumble, not too large or they won't crisp properly. Prep your onion, garlic, and green onions. Having everything ready before you start cooking makes the process smooth and fast.

## Crisp the Spam

Heat a large skillet or wok over **medium-high heat**. Add the neutral oil, then toss in the Spam cubes. **Stir-fry for 3-4 minutes, stirring occasionally**, until the edges turn golden-brown and slightly crispy. Spam releases its own fat as it cooks, which adds incredible flavor to the rice later. Don't rush this step—the crispy, caramelized edges are what make this dish addictive.

Once the Spam is nicely browned, push it to one side of the pan or remove it temporarily to a plate if your pan is small.

## Sauté Aromatics and Rice

In the same pan (with the Spam fat), add the diced onion and white parts of the green onions. **Stir-fry for 1-2 minutes until softened and fragrant**. Add the minced garlic and cook for another 30 seconds—watch carefully so it doesn't burn.

Now add the cold rice. **Break up any remaining clumps with your spatula and stir-fry for 2-3 minutes**, mixing well so every grain gets coated with the flavorful fat and aromatics. You want the rice to heat through and start to separate into individual grains. If it's sticking, your heat might be too high—adjust to medium.

## Season and Combine

Return the crispy Spam to the pan (if you removed it). Drizzle the soy sauce and sesame oil over the rice, then sprinkle in the sugar and a few good cracks of black pepper. **Toss everything together quickly for 1-2 minutes** until the rice is evenly coated and lightly glazed. The sugar balances the saltiness of the Spam and soy sauce—don't skip it.

Taste and adjust: if you want more savory depth, add a splash more soy sauce; if it feels flat, add a bit more sesame oil. Toss in the green parts of the green onions and stir once more.

> 💡 **Tip:** Don't over-soy-sauce the rice. Start with the listed amount—you can always add more, but you can't take it back. Spam is already salty, so go lighter than you think.

## Fry the Eggs (Optional but Highly Recommended)

In a separate small pan, fry your eggs **sunny-side-up with slightly runny yolks**. The rich, runny yolk mixing into the fried rice is a game-changer. If you prefer, you can scramble the eggs directly into the rice before adding the Spam—this is also a common variation and works great.

## Serving and Variations

Scoop the fried rice into bowls and **top each serving with a fried egg**. Garnish with toasted sesame seeds and seaweed flakes if you have them—they add a subtle ocean-y flavor that's very Korean. Serve immediately while hot.

**Substitutions and tips:** If you can't find Spam or want a lighter option, diced ham or cooked bacon works well (though it won't taste quite as authentically Korean). No sesame oil? It's hard to substitute, but a tiny drizzle of toasted peanut oil adds a nutty note. For a veggie boost, toss in frozen peas, corn, or finely diced carrots when you sauté the onions. And if you *do* have kimchi on hand, a handful of chopped kimchi added with the rice takes this to another level—but it's absolutely delicious without it, which is why this version is perfect for anyone cooking Korean food far from a Korean grocery store.
